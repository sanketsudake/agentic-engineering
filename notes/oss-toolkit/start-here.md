- WHAT THIS IS
- Every layer of the open source AI agent stack in one file. Over 400 entries across 20 sheets, each with what it does, who it is for, the licence and a working link.
- Everything listed here is open source or open weight. You can read the code, run it on your own machine and ship it without asking anyone for a key.
- Built for the engineer who is tired of tutorial hopping and wants one place to decide what to learn, what to install and what to build next.
- HOW TO USE IT
- 1.  New to agents. Go to sheet 16, follow the twelve week roadmap, and only open the catalog sheets when the roadmap tells you to.
- 2.  Building something now. Go to sheet 15, copy the stack recipe that matches your budget, then look up each tool in its own sheet.
- 3.  Preparing for interviews. Sheet 18 has forty real questions. Sheet 17 has fourteen projects. Do two projects, then answer the questions out loud.
- 4.  Choosing a tool. Filter any catalog sheet by Difficulty and Adoption. When two options are close, pick the one with higher adoption. Documentation and answers matter more than features.
- 5.  Every Link column is clickable. Every sheet has filters on the header row and frozen panes, so scroll freely.
- HOW TO READ THE COLUMNS
- Difficulty | Beginner means you can be productive in a day. Intermediate needs a week. Advanced needs infrastructure knowledge.
- Adoption | Very High and High mean a large community, so your error message is already on Stack Overflow. Emerging means promising but you may be the first to hit a bug.
- Licence | MIT and Apache 2.0 are safe for commercial use. AGPL and SSPL have obligations if you host the software as a service. Check before you build a business on it.
- Link | Points to the source repository or official documentation, never to a paid landing page.

- FIVE THINGS WORTH KNOWING BEFORE YOU START
- 1.  Most production systems are workflows with a small agentic core. Give the model freedom only where the path genuinely cannot be written in advance.
- 2.  Accuracy compounds downward. Ninety five percent per step is thirty six percent over twenty steps. Short loops beat clever prompts.
- 3.  Context engineering beats prompt engineering. What you put in the window at each step decides quality far more than how you phrase the instruction.
- 4.  Wire up tracing before you wire up features. You cannot debug what you cannot see, and every agent bug is a trace you have not read yet.
- 5.  Adoption beats features when choosing a tool. A slightly worse library with ten times the users will cost you far less time.
